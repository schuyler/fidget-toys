How to play

  • Bring three relics home from the three core hexes at the centre of the galaxy, or take every one of the CPU's settlements. You're blue and you move first.
  • Jump. Tap one of your stacks, then a hex: you see the odds of landing on target and every hex you might scatter to. Tap the hex again (or Jump) to go. A one-hex hop always lands; longer jumps can miss.
  • Missing scatters you. The further the jump, the worse the miss. Fleets that scatter far are lost on the way, and a stack is lost entirely if it lands off the map, in the nucleus, or out of sight of your settlements (red ✕ in the preview).
  • Safe range is 2 hexes, more with Reach techs. Longer jumps are allowed only from your own settlement, and miss more. Landing on target at one of your settlements lets a stack jump again, so a network of settlements carries fleets far in one turn.
  • Hex values are hidden until you land there. Bright arm stars are worth 3–5, the arm's edge 1–3, the void between arms 0–1.
  • Settle with 2 fleets that started the turn in a hex. Each settlement makes RP equal to its value each turn. Spend a fleet to grow it, RP to build fleets, or fleets to research a tech.
  • Techs each add +1 to one thing: Reach (range and sight), Focus (jump rolls), Industry (fleets built per turn) or Force (combat and sieges). A delivered relic counts as +1 to all four.
  • Relics. Land in a core hex and hold it through the CPU's turn to pick up its relic, then jump it to any of your settlements. Jumps into or out of the nucleus that miss are fatal. Carriers can't chain-jump.
  • Fights happen when you jump onto a rival stack, and go on until one side is gone. Land on an undefended rival settlement to try to take it; stay to keep trying, and it makes nothing for them meanwhile.
  • Dust lanes (brown walls) block sight and jumps, except one-hex hops and jumps between your own settlements. Beacons (bright stars) make jumps aimed at them more accurate.
  • Upkeep: up to 3 fleets in a hex are free; each one beyond costs 1 RP a turn. You see the CPU's stacks only roughly: 1, 2–3, 4–6 or 7+.
